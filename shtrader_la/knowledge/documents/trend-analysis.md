# Trend Analysis
tags: trend, moving average, momentum, pullback, timeframe

A trend is a bias, not a prediction. Trading with the trend means accepting a higher base rate of continuation than reversal, which improves win rate without guaranteeing any individual trade.

Moving averages summarise trend without forecasting it. Price above a rising 50-period average with the 200-period below it describes an established uptrend; averages crossing describes a change that has already happened. Averages lag by construction — using them as entry triggers in a range produces repeated whipsaw losses, which is why they belong in the context step, not the trigger step.

Pullback entries into a trend give the best risk geometry: the stop can sit just beyond the pullback's origin while the target remains the trend extension, producing natural multi-R setups. Breakout entries pay a worse price for higher confirmation, so they need either a tighter structural stop or smaller size to keep risk constant.

Momentum divergence — price making a new extreme while an oscillator does not — is a warning about the strength of a leg, not a reversal signal. Divergence can persist for many bars. Act on it only when structure confirms with a break of the most recent counter-swing.

Multi-timeframe alignment is a practical filter: define direction on the higher timeframe, define the zone on the intermediate, and trigger on the lower. When the three disagree, there is no trade — the absence of a setup is a valid outcome of analysis.
