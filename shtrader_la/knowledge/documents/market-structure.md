# Market Structure
tags: market structure, swing, break of structure, support resistance, range

Market structure is the sequence of swing highs and swing lows that describes who is in control. An uptrend prints higher highs and higher lows; a downtrend prints lower highs and lower lows; a range prints highs and lows that fail to extend. Structure is fractal — the same reading applies on the 5-minute and the weekly chart — so a trade must state which timeframe defines its structure and which defines its entry.

A break of structure occurs when price closes beyond the swing point that defined the prior leg. Until that close, a move beyond the level is a wick and is often liquidity being taken before continuation in the original direction. Waiting for the close is what separates a structural break from a stop hunt.

Support and resistance are zones, not lines. They are drawn from the region where price previously reversed or consolidated, and their strength comes from how many times they produced a reaction and how recent those reactions were. When a level breaks and price retests it from the other side, the level flips role: prior resistance becomes support.

Ranges end in one of two ways: expansion out of the boundary with a close and follow-through, or a false break that reclaims the boundary within a candle or two. Trading the boundary requires a stop beyond the false-break zone, which is usually wider than traders expect and must be reflected in position size.

Higher-timeframe context outranks lower-timeframe signals. A long setup on the 15-minute inside a daily downtrend is a counter-trend trade and should be sized and targeted as one: smaller size, nearer targets, faster invalidation.
